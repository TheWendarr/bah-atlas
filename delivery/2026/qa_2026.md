Its rent is HUD's Small Area Fair Market Rent for the ZIP code, scaled so that the MHA's typical HUD rent equals its typical Zillow rent. HUD's figures are smoothed, so differences between ZIP codes are likely understated. |
| `zip_tax_insurance` | row | ZIP code tax and insurance | Property tax is this ZIP code's effective rate from the Census American Community Survey where one is published, and insurance its median premium per policy from the Treasury Federal Insurance Office where published; otherwise its MHA's figure is used. |
| `statutory_target` | global | How adequacy is measured | By law, BAH equals the local cost of adequate housing minus a member cost share of 5% of the national average for the pay grade. An adequacy ratio of 1.00 means BAH plus that cost share exactly covers the local benchmark. |
| `market_rents` | global | Market rents, not DoD survey rents | Local rent is the median of Zillow's rent index across the MHA's ZIP codes, a measure of typical asking rents for all homes. DoD's own survey prices specific dwelling types for each pay grade. |
| `benchmark_timing` | global | Benchmarks differ in timing | BAH rates are for the BAH year shown, while each benchmark covers its own period. The data windows list the months and years used. |
| `zip_zcta_dropout` | global | Boundaries are approximate | Area outlines are built from Census ZIP Code Tabulation Areas. PO-box and single-site ZIP codes have no tabulation area and are not drawn, although the areas they belong to still have outlines. Tabulation areas also leave out much unpopulated land, mostly in the West, so those places appear blank on the map. |
| `home_type_benchmarks` | global | How home types are priced | Rent: Zillow's typical rent for an area is taken to stand for a 2-bedroom townhouse, and other bedroom counts are scaled from it by the area's HUD Fair Market Rent bedroom ratios. Apartments and single-family homes are priced from Zillow's multifamily and single-family rents, compared at the same number of bedrooms: Zillow's typical single-family rental is a 3-bedroom home, so that size difference is taken out before bedrooms are added. A single-family home is never priced below a townhouse with the same bedrooms. Zillow publishes those two rent series only by metro area, so the building-type adjustment is a metro-wide ratio. Buy: Zillow's typical home value stands for a 3-bedroom home, and other bedroom counts are scaled by Zillow's bedroom-count home values. Apartments use condo values, and Zillow publishes no townhouse values, so a townhouse is priced halfway between the area's condo and single-family values at the same bedrooms. Where condos cost more than houses of the same size, all three are priced at the condo level, so cost never falls as the home type rises. Homeowners insurance is the area's average premium for every home type. |
