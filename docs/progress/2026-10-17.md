**What changed and what the results show** *(author)*:

B12–B15 test each MHA's BAH against its statutory design: (BAH + the member cost share) ÷ the local benchmark cost, where 1.00 means BAH meets 37 U.S.C. 403(b)(3) exactly.

- **E-5 renters with dependents** (the primary profile): **44 of the 289 MHAs** with a rent benchmark fall short of the design. The median adequacy ratio is **1.136**, so in the typical MHA, DoD's implied local cost sits about 14% above the utilities-adjusted ZORI benchmark.
- **The flat-95% reading** would have called 52 MHAs short. The 8 it adds are lower-cost MHAs that meet the statutory design: AR012, ID084, KS101, MS168, NC177, TX283, WA313 and WV454. This is the effect the change of test variable in part 4 corrects.
- **O-3 renters with dependents** fall short in 1 MHA (median 1.431).
- **Buy mode** shows a different picture. A VA-financed purchase of the typical home (PITI plus utilities, at 2026 mortgage rates) exceeds BAH plus the cost share in 267 of 289 MHAs for E-5 (median 0.779) and in 159 for O-3 (median 0.985). Buy-mode figures use national placeholder property tax and insurance until A8 lands.

| B12–B15 | Decision | A flat 0.95 target mis-scores BAH's own formula. 37 U.S.C. 403(b)(3) subtracts the same dollar cost share (5% of the *national* average cost) at every location, so BAH equals exactly 95% of local cost only where local cost equals the national mean. Cheap MHAs score short and expensive MHAs score over by design, and because cost levels cluster, the flat-95% gap would carry spatial autocorrelation into the 2.6 tests by construction. | Changed the test variable to **adequacy_gap** = (BAH + cost share) ÷ local cost − 1, target 1.00, using DTMO's 2026 out-of-pocket amounts (E-5 w/ deps $126, O-3 w/ deps $151). The flat-95% gap is kept as a sensitivity column, and the results file lists which MHAs change status between the two readings.
